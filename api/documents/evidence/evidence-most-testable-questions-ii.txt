Evidence Most Testable Questions II
===================================

Subject: Evidence
Type: Practice Questions
Source: https://supertoadman.github.io/Wilmington-University-corner/subjects/evidence/evidence-most-testable-questions-ii/

60 more five-choice questions on the most testable material from Weeks 1–15, in two sets of 30, testing different rule points from the first collection. About a third is hearsay, and it adds jury secrecy, competency, Rule 411, Rule 106, and compulsory process. Every question uses new facts, and every choice is explained.

## Set 1 · Q1 · Jury Secrecy and Competency — Rule 606(b): outside influence
After a guilty verdict, the defense offers juror testimony to overturn it. Which testimony may the court consider?

A. The foreperson pressured two holdouts until they changed their votes.
B. A juror described her own experience of being robbed during deliberations.
C. A juror says she voted guilty only because she was exhausted and wanted to go home.
D. A juror says several jurors drank heavily at lunch and slept through testimony.
E. A bailiff told the jurors during a break that the defendant had confessed.

Answer: E
- A: Pressure among jurors is a statement or incident during deliberations, which Rule 606(b)(1) bars.
- B: A juror's own life experience isn't extraneous information (Warger), and it was a statement during deliberations.
- C: Rule 606(b)(1) bars testimony about the effect of anything on a juror's vote or mental processes.
- D: Tanner treats intoxication and sleeping as internal matters, no more an outside influence than a virus or lack of sleep.
- E: Correct. A bailiff's remark brings extraneous prejudicial information or an outside influence to the jury, so Rule 606(b)(2) lets a juror testify about it.
Rule: Rule 606(b) bars juror testimony about deliberations, votes, and mental processes. A juror may testify about extraneous prejudicial information, an outside influence, or a mistake on the verdict form. Internal vs. external turns on the nature of the matter (Tanner).

## Set 1 · Q2 · Relevance and Rule 403 — Rule 403: undisputed fact
In a murder trial, the only disputed issue is who committed the killing. The defense concedes the cause and manner of death. The State offers graphic autopsy photographs of the opened skull. The defense objects. What is the best analysis?

A. The photos are relevant, but their value is low here and may be substantially outweighed.
B. The photos are irrelevant, because evidence on an undisputed fact fails Rule 401.
C. The photos must be admitted, because the State may always prove each element its own way.
D. The photos must be excluded, because gruesome images are unfairly prejudicial as a matter of law.
E. The photos come in, because a limiting instruction cures any emotional reaction to them.

Answer: A
- A: Correct. Under State v. Bocharski, an undisputed fact shrinks probative value on the Rule 403 scale without making the evidence irrelevant. The two autopsy photos there served mainly to inflame.
- B: The fact need not be disputed under Rule 401 (ACN). A brick is not a wall, and the problem arises under Rule 403.
- C: That is the general rule of Old Chief, but it doesn't remove the Rule 403 balance.
- D: Rule 403 has no per se rule. Some gruesome photos in State v. Bocharski were properly admitted.
- E: The likely effectiveness of an instruction is one factor. It doesn't automatically resolve the balance.
Rule: An undisputed fact can still be relevant, but Rule 403 discounts its probative value. Exclusion requires that the value be substantially outweighed by unfair prejudice or another listed danger (State v. Bocharski).

## Set 1 · Q3 · Specialized Relevance Rules — Rule 411: bias exception
In a slip-and-fall suit, the store's key witness is an investigator who testifies the floor was dry. On cross, the plaintiff seeks to show the investigator works for the store's liability insurer. The store objects under Rule 411. What is the most likely ruling?

A. Sustained, because Rule 411 bars all mention of liability insurance anywhere in a negligence trial.
B. Overruled, because the evidence shows the witness's bias, with an instruction on request.
C. Overruled, because being insured makes it more likely the store acted carelessly.
D. Sustained, because bias may be shown only by the witness's own financial stake.
E. Overruled, because Rule 411 protects only parties who have no liability insurance.

Answer: B
- A: Rule 411 bars insurance evidence only to prove negligence or wrongful conduct. It allows other purposes.
- B: Correct. Rule 411 expressly permits insurance evidence for another purpose, such as a witness's bias or prejudice. Rule 105 limits the jury's use on request.
- C: That is the inference Rule 411 forbids.
- D: Employment by an interested insurer is a recognized source of bias. No rule limits bias to a direct financial stake.
- E: Rule 411 applies whether a person was or was not insured.
Rule: Rule 411 bars proof of insurance to show negligence or wrongful conduct. It may come in for another purpose, such as bias, agency, ownership, or control.

## Set 1 · Q4 · Character Evidence — Zackowitz: weapons unconnected to the crime
Dunn is charged with stabbing a man outside a bar with a pocketknife, which police recovered. The State offers a collection of a dozen combat knives found in Dunn's garage, unconnected to the stabbing, to show he is a dangerous man. What is the most likely ruling?

A. Admitted, because possessing knives is not a crime, so Rule 404(b) doesn't apply.
B. Admitted, subject to a limiting instruction that the jury may not punish him for them.
C. Excluded, because a defendant's lawful property can never be used against him at trial.
D. Admitted, because weapons in the home show he had the means to commit the crime.
E. Excluded, because the collection proves only character and has no non-propensity link.

Answer: E
- A: Rule 404(b) reaches any other crime, wrong, or act, and Rule 404(a)(1) bars character evidence generally.
- B: An instruction can't rescue evidence whose only purpose is forbidden.
- C: This is too broad. Items tied to the crime, or to preparation for it, can come in.
- D: The knife used was recovered, so the collection adds nothing on means. Its only force is that Dunn is a dangerous type.
- E: Correct. As in People v. Zackowitz, weapons unconnected to the crime prove only a dangerous character. Rule 404(b)(1) bars that use.
Rule: Other acts need a non-character route under Rule 404(b)(2). Evidence that shows only the defendant is a dangerous type falls within the propensity ban (People v. Zackowitz).

## Set 1 · Q5 · Character Evidence — Plan vs. pattern
In which case is the other act best offered to prove a plan under Rule 404(b)(2)?

A. A child-abuse defendant has abused other children in the same church.
B. A bank-robbery defendant stole a van the day before and used it for the getaway.
C. A forgery defendant has a long history of signing other people's checks.
D. A bank-robbery defendant robbed three other banks over the past five years.
E. A shoplifting defendant has shoplifted from similar stores many times before.

Answer: B
- A: This is the argument State v. Kirsch rejected: a systematic course of conduct isn't a plan.
- B: Correct. Plan requires acts that are steps toward one overall scheme. Stealing a getaway van is preparation for this robbery.
- C: A history of similar acts is propensity unless linked to one scheme.
- D: Repeated robberies show a pattern, not parts of one scheme (State v. Kirsch).
- E: A mere pattern is insufficient to establish a plan. This shows only a tendency to shoplift.
Rule: Plan under Rule 404(b)(2) requires that the acts be parts of one overall scheme. A pattern or systematic course of conduct isn't enough (State v. Kirsch).

## Set 1 · Q6 · Character Evidence — Rule 404(a)(2): criminal cases only
In a civil battery suit, the defendant calls a witness to testify to the defendant's excellent reputation for peacefulness, offered to show he didn't strike the plaintiff. The plaintiff objects. What is the correct ruling?

A. Overruled, because a defendant may always offer a pertinent trait.
B. Overruled, because Rule 405(a) allows reputation testimony.
C. Sustained, because the defendant hasn't testified yet.
D. Overruled, because battery makes character an essential element.
E. None of the above.

Answer: E
- A: That exception, Rule 404(a)(2)(A), belongs to a defendant in a criminal case.
- B: Rule 405(a) governs the method of proof only after character is admissible.
- C: Testifying has no bearing here. Character evidence offered as propensity is barred either way.
- D: Peacefulness isn't an element of a battery claim, so Rule 405(b) doesn't apply.
- E: Correct. The testimony should be excluded. Rule 404(a)(1) bars character evidence to prove action in conformity, and the Rule 404(a)(2) exceptions apply only in criminal cases.
Rule: Only the accused can open the door to character under Rule 404(a)(2), and only in a criminal case. In civil cases the propensity ban holds.

## Set 1 · Q7 · Character Evidence — Rule 404(a)(2)(C): first aggressor in homicide
Fenn is charged with murder. He offers no character evidence, but an eyewitness testifies for the defense that the victim attacked first. The prosecutor then calls a witness to testify to the victim's reputation for peacefulness. What is the correct ruling?

A. Excluded, because an alleged victim's character can never be proved by the State.
B. Excluded, because Fenn has not offered any evidence of the victim's character.
C. Excluded, because Rule 405(a) allows only opinion testimony for this purpose.
D. Admitted, because evidence the victim was the first aggressor opens this door.
E. Admitted, because the State may always offer the victim's character in its case.

Answer: D
- A: Rule 404(a)(2)(C) and the rebuttal right in Rule 404(a)(2)(B) both allow it.
- B: Rule 404(a)(2)(C) doesn't need character evidence from the defense. Any evidence that the victim was the first aggressor triggers it.
- C: Rule 405(a) allows reputation or opinion.
- D: Correct. In a homicide case, Rule 404(a)(2)(C) lets the prosecutor offer the victim's trait of peacefulness to rebut evidence that the victim was the first aggressor.
- E: The prosecution can't go first. It needs the trigger the rule describes.
Rule: In a homicide case, any evidence that the victim was the first aggressor lets the prosecutor offer the victim's peacefulness, under Rule 404(a)(2)(C).

## Set 1 · Q8 · Impeachment and the Rape Shield — Rule 609(a)(1): maximum punishment controls
A witness in a civil trial was convicted of felony theft eight years ago. The crime was punishable by up to three years, but the witness received only six months of probation. Theft of this kind has no element of dishonesty or false statement. Which is correct?

A. It can't be used, because theft isn't a crime of false statement.
B. It must be admitted with no balancing, because it is a felony.
C. It can't be used, because the sentence the court actually imposed was under one year.
D. It comes in unless Rule 403 requires exclusion, because the maximum exceeds a year.
E. It comes in only if probative value substantially outweighs prejudice.

Answer: D
- A: That defeats only Rule 609(a)(2). Rule 609(a)(1) covers any crime punishable by more than one year.
- B: No balancing applies only to crimen falsi under Rule 609(a)(2). This conviction is subject to Rule 403.
- C: What counts is the maximum punishment, not the sentence imposed.
- D: Correct. Rule 609(a)(1)(A) applies because the crime was punishable by more than one year. For a civil witness, the conviction must be admitted, subject to Rule 403.
- E: The reverse balance belongs to Rule 609(b). This conviction is under 10 years old.
Rule: Rule 609(a)(1) looks at the maximum punishment in the convicting jurisdiction, not the sentence actually imposed.

## Set 1 · Q9 · Impeachment and the Rape Shield — Luce: preserving a Rule 609 ruling
Before trial, the judge rules that the government may impeach Boyd with a prior conviction if he testifies. Boyd decides not to testify and is convicted. On appeal, he challenges the in limine ruling. What is the most likely result?

A. The claim isn't preserved, because Boyd never testified.
B. Reversal, because the ruling effectively kept him off the stand.
C. Review on the merits, because a definitive ruling preserves the claim.
D. Remand, so the trial judge can decide what Boyd would have said.
E. Reversal only if he proves he would have testified otherwise.

Answer: A
- A: Correct. Under Luce, a defendant must testify to challenge an in limine Rule 609 ruling.
- B: Under Luce the harm is wholly speculative when the defendant doesn't testify.
- C: A definitive ruling doesn't preserve this claim. Luce requires the defendant to testify.
- D: Luce doesn't call for a remand. The challenge simply isn't preserved.
- E: No offer of proof substitutes for testifying under Luce.
Rule: A defendant must testify to preserve a challenge to an in limine Rule 609 ruling (Luce).

## Set 1 · Q10 · Impeachment and the Rape Shield — Ohler: drawing the sting
(Varies question 1.9.)
Same facts, except Boyd testifies and, to soften the blow, tells the jury about the conviction himself on direct. The government never mentions it. On appeal, he challenges the in limine ruling. What is the most likely result?

A. The claim is preserved, because he testified as Luce requires.
B. The claim is preserved, because he only responded to the ruling.
C. The claim is preserved if he objected again during his direct.
D. The claim is waived, because he introduced the conviction himself.
E. Reversal, because the ruling forced him to reveal his conviction.

Answer: D
- A: Testifying is necessary but not sufficient. Ohler adds that he can't introduce the conviction himself.
- B: Ohler rejected this reasoning, even though the defendant was responding to an adverse ruling.
- C: A further objection doesn't change who introduced the evidence.
- D: Correct. Under Ohler, a defendant who preemptively introduces his own conviction on direct can't challenge its admission on appeal.
- E: The conviction came from his own testimony, so there is no error by the court to review.
Rule: With Luce and Ohler together, a defendant preserves a Rule 609 objection only by testifying and letting the prosecution elicit the conviction.

## Set 1 · Q11 · Definition of Hearsay — Verbal acts
A restaurant owner is charged with bribing a health inspector. A waiter testifies that he heard the inspector tell the owner, "Pay me $500 and this violation disappears." The inspector doesn't testify. The defense objects on hearsay grounds. What is the best ruling?

A. Sustained, because the inspector is not on the stand and can't be cross-examined.
B. Sustained, unless the prosecution first proves the inspector is unavailable.
C. Overruled, because the statement is a present sense impression of the visit.
D. Overruled, because the waiter personally heard the words and can be cross-examined.
E. Overruled, because the words are a verbal act with independent legal significance.

Answer: E
- A: The inspector's absence matters only if the words are offered for their truth. They aren't.
- B: Unavailability is a condition for Rule 804. The statement isn't hearsay at all.
- C: An offer doesn't describe an event, and no exception is needed for words that aren't hearsay.
- D: The waiter's knowledge covers only what was said. That doesn't answer a hearsay objection to a statement offered for truth.
- E: Correct. The offer has legal effect because it was said, whether or not it was sincere. It isn't offered to prove the truth of an assertion under Rule 801(c)(2).
Rule: Verbal acts such as contract words, threats, and offers have legal effect because they were said. They aren't hearsay under Rule 801(c).

## Set 1 · Q12 · Definition of Hearsay — Machine output
To prove a delivery truck was at a loading dock at 2:14 a.m., the plaintiff offers a printout automatically generated by the truck's GPS unit. No person entered the data. The defendant objects on hearsay grounds. What is the best ruling?

A. Overruled, because the printout is self-authenticating under Rule 902.
B. Sustained, because the printout is an out-of-court assertion offered for its truth.
C. Sustained, unless a technician testifies to the unit's records under Rule 803(6).
D. Overruled, because the printout is not a statement made by a person.
E. Overruled, because any computer record is a business record.

Answer: D
- A: Authentication is a separate question from hearsay, and the objection here is hearsay.
- B: Rule 801(a) requires a person's assertion. A machine's automatic output isn't one.
- C: No exception is needed if the output isn't hearsay.
- D: Correct. Hearsay requires a statement by a person under Rule 801(a)–(b). Machine output isn't hearsay, though it must still be authenticated, as under Rule 901(b)(9).
- E: Rule 803(6) has requirements of its own, and data aren't business records just because they are on a computer.
Rule: Machine output isn't a person's statement, so it isn't hearsay. The proponent still authenticates it, typically by showing the process produces an accurate result under Rule 901(b)(9).

## Set 1 · Q13 · Opposing Party and Prior Statements — Adoption by silence
At a diner, before any arrest, Gale's business partner says to her in front of two friends, "You and I both know we skimmed that cash." Gale, who plainly heard him, stares at her coffee and says nothing. At her fraud trial, the government offers the exchange. What is the most likely ruling?

A. Admitted, because a business partner's remarks are always authorized.
B. Excluded, because silence can never be a statement.
C. Admitted, if the circumstances naturally called for a denial she could have given.
D. Excluded, because using her silence violates her constitutional right to remain silent.
E. Admitted, because anything said in a party's presence is her own statement.

Answer: C
- A: A partner's authority under Rule 801(d)(2)(C) needs proof. Nothing shows authority to confess fraud.
- B: Rule 801(d)(2)(B) covers a statement a party adopted or believed to be true, and silence can show adoption.
- C: Correct. Adoption by silence under Rule 801(d)(2)(B) requires that she heard and understood, was free to respond, the circumstances naturally called for a denial, and she didn't deny.
- D: Doyle concerns silence after Miranda warnings. Silence with a private accuser before arrest is unaffected.
- E: Presence isn't enough. The elements of adoption must be met.
Rule: Silence can adopt a statement under Rule 801(d)(2)(B) if the party heard and understood it, was free to respond, the circumstances naturally called for a denial, and the party didn't deny it.

## Set 1 · Q14 · Opposing Party and Prior Statements — Doyle: post-Miranda silence
After arrest and Miranda warnings, Hart says nothing to police. At trial, he testifies that he was framed. The prosecutor asks, "If you were framed, why didn't you tell the officers that night?" The defense objects. What is the correct ruling?

A. Overruled, because Rule 613 allows impeachment with an inconsistent omission.
B. Overruled, because silence in the face of an accusation is an adoptive admission.
C. Sustained, because no silence by a defendant may ever be mentioned at trial.
D. Overruled, because a testifying defendant may be impeached by any prior silence.
E. Sustained, because silence after Miranda warnings can't be used to impeach him.

Answer: E
- A: Rule 613 doesn't override the due-process rule of Doyle.
- B: Adoption by silence is limited when the accusation follows Miranda warnings.
- C: This is too broad. Pre-arrest silence may impeach, as may silence before Miranda warnings.
- D: Doyle limits this. Pre-arrest and pre-Miranda silence may impeach; post-Miranda silence may not.
- E: Correct. Doyle holds that using post-Miranda silence to impeach violates due process. The silence is insolubly ambiguous, and the warnings imply no penalty.
Rule: Post-Miranda silence may not be used to impeach (Doyle). Pre-arrest and pre-Miranda silence may be.

## Set 1 · Q15 · Opposing Party and Prior Statements — Rule 801(d)(1)(C): identification
A mugging victim picked Lowe from a lineup two days after the crime. At trial she testifies and answers questions, but says she can no longer remember what her attacker looked like. The officer who ran the lineup testifies that she picked Lowe. What is the most likely ruling?

A. Excluded, because an out-of-court identification must be repeated in court.
B. Admitted, because she testified and identified a person she perceived earlier.
C. Excluded, because her memory loss means she is not subject to cross-examination.
D. Admitted, but only to impeach her testimony about her present memory.
E. Excluded, because the officer has no personal knowledge of the mugging.

Answer: B
- A: The rule admits the identification even if the witness now can't or doesn't identify in court.
- B: Correct. Rule 801(d)(1)(C) makes the identification not hearsay when the declarant testifies and is subject to cross. Under United States v. Owens, memory loss doesn't change that.
- C: United States v. Owens rejected this. Memory loss doesn't make a testifying witness unavailable for cross.
- D: The identification is substantive evidence under Rule 801(d)(1)(C), not just impeachment.
- E: The officer has personal knowledge of what happened at the lineup, which is what he reports.
Rule: Rule 801(d)(1)(C) admits a prior identification if the declarant testifies and is subject to cross, even with memory loss or a misidentification in court (United States v. Owens).

## Set 1 · Q16 · Hearsay Exceptions — Rule 803(1): no stress required
Driving calmly and talking to her sister by phone, Priya says, "That blue truck just went through the red light." Her sister testifies to this in a later suit. Priya was not upset, and nothing shows the sister saw anything. What is the best basis for admission?

A. Rule 803(2), because a traffic violation is a startling event.
B. Rule 803(3), because it reflects her then-existing state of mind.
C. No exception, because the sister didn't perceive the event herself.
D. Rule 803(1), because it describes the event just as she perceived it.
E. Rule 804(b)(3), because it exposes the truck driver to liability.

Answer: D
- A: Priya was calm, so she wasn't under the stress of excitement that Rule 803(2) requires.
- B: Rule 803(3) excludes a statement of memory or belief offered to prove the fact believed.
- C: The personal-knowledge requirement applies to the declarant. The sister needs only to have heard the statement.
- D: Correct. Rule 803(1) covers a statement describing an event made while or immediately after perceiving it. No stress is required.
- E: Rule 804(b)(3) looks to the declarant's own interest, and Priya isn't shown to be unavailable.
Rule: Rule 803(1) requires timing, not excitement. Rule 803(2) requires stress, not a strict time limit. Personal knowledge applies to the declarant.

## Set 1 · Q17 · Hearsay Exceptions — Rule 803(3): memory and the will exception
Which statement, offered for its truth, is admissible under Rule 803(3)?

A. "My husband poisoned my coffee last night," offered in his trial to prove that he did it.
B. "I paid the rent on the first," offered to prove the rent was paid.
C. "Last spring I signed a will leaving all to Ann," offered in a dispute over that will.
D. "I believe the mechanic fixed the brakes," offered to prove they were fixed.
E. "I remember the light being green," offered to prove the light was green.

Answer: C
- A: As in Shepard, this looks backward to another person's act. Rule 803(3) excludes it.
- B: A statement of memory offered to prove the fact remembered falls outside Rule 803(3).
- C: Correct. Rule 803(3) excludes memory or belief offered to prove the fact remembered, except as it relates to the declarant's will.
- D: A statement of belief offered to prove the fact believed is excluded.
- E: This is memory offered to prove the fact remembered, the core exclusion in Rule 803(3).
Rule: Rule 803(3) covers then-existing state of mind, but not memory or belief offered to prove the fact remembered, unless it relates to the validity or terms of the declarant's will (Shepard).

## Set 1 · Q18 · Hearsay Exceptions — Rule 804(a): who is unavailable
Which declarant is NOT unavailable under Rule 804(a)?

A. One who refuses to testify in spite of a court order to do so.
B. One who is excused from testifying by a court's privilege ruling.
C. One who takes the stand and testifies to not remembering the subject.
D. One who has died or is too ill to appear at the trial or hearing.
E. One whom the proponent paid to leave the country to avoid testifying.

Answer: E
- A: Rule 804(a)(2) covers this declarant.
- B: Rule 804(a)(1) covers this declarant.
- C: Rule 804(a)(3) covers this declarant.
- D: Rule 804(a)(4) covers this declarant.
- E: Correct. Rule 804(a) doesn't apply if the proponent procured or wrongfully caused the unavailability in order to prevent the declarant from testifying.
Rule: Rule 804(a) lists privilege, refusal, lack of memory, death or illness, and absence despite reasonable efforts. A proponent who wrongfully caused the absence can't rely on it.

## Set 1 · Q19 · Hearsay Exceptions — Rule 807: near misses
A hardware supplier's sales ledger narrowly fails Rule 803(6) because its sole owner has died and no qualified witness can lay the foundation. The ledger's entries are corroborated by bank deposits, and it is the best available proof of the sales. The proponent gave reasonable written notice. Can it come in under Rule 807?

A. No, because a statement that narrowly misses an exception can't use Rule 807.
B. Yes, because the owner's death by itself satisfies Rule 804(b) and so Rule 807.
C. Yes, if it is trustworthy and the most probative evidence reasonably obtainable.
D. No, because Rule 807 applies only to statements of unavailable public officials.
E. Yes, because any hearsay that seems reliable to the judge is admissible.

Answer: C
- A: The 2019 amendment ended the near-miss debate. Rule 807 asks whether the statement is not admissible under Rule 803 or 804.
- B: Death shows unavailability only. It satisfies no exception.
- C: Correct. Rule 807(a) requires sufficient guarantees of trustworthiness and that the statement be more probative than other evidence obtainable with reasonable efforts. Near misses qualify (United States v. Laster).
- D: Rule 807 has no such limit.
- E: Rule 807(a) has two requirements, and courts use it rarely. General reliability isn't enough.
Rule: Rule 807(a) requires trustworthiness, considering the totality of circumstances and corroboration, and comparative necessity. Rule 807(b) requires reasonable written notice. A near miss may still be considered.

## Set 1 · Q20 · Hearsay Exceptions — Rule 806: attacking a declarant
The government introduces a coconspirator's recorded statement under Rule 801(d)(2)(E). The coconspirator doesn't testify. The defense offers proof of the coconspirator's conviction for perjury five years ago. The government objects that the declarant never took the stand. What is the correct ruling?

A. Overruled, because a perjury conviction is substantive proof of innocence.
B. Overruled, because the declarant may be impeached as if the declarant had testified.
C. Sustained, because Rule 806 doesn't apply to coconspirators' statements.
D. Sustained, because the declarant wasn't given a chance to explain or deny the conviction.
E. Sustained, because only a testifying witness may be impeached.

Answer: B
- A: The conviction is relevant to the declarant's credibility. It proves nothing directly about guilt.
- B: Correct. Under Rule 806, once a statement under Rule 801(d)(2)(C), (D), or (E) or a hearsay statement is admitted, the declarant's credibility may be attacked as if the declarant had testified. Rule 609 convictions may be used.
- C: Rule 806 expressly names Rule 801(d)(2)(C), (D), and (E) statements.
- D: Rule 806 removes the foundation requirement of Rule 613(b) for an absent declarant.
- E: Rule 806 exists to allow impeachment of absent declarants.
Rule: Rule 806 lets a party attack an absent declarant's credibility as if the declarant had testified, including with Rule 608 and Rule 609 evidence. The Rule 613(b) foundation isn't required.

## Set 1 · Q21 · Confrontation, Bruton, and the Defense — Bryant: emergency to the public
Officers find a man mortally shot in a parking lot. He says, "Deshawn shot me at the bar down the street." The shooter hasn't been found, and no one knows where he is. The man dies before trial, and the prosecution offers the statement. Is it testimonial?

A. No, because the objective primary purpose was to meet an ongoing emergency.
B. No, because he was dying, so every dying declaration falls outside the Clause.
C. Yes, because police asked questions at the scene, which is an interrogation.
D. Yes, because all statements to police about past events are testimonial.
E. No, because the Confrontation Clause applies only to statements in court.

Answer: A
- A: Correct. Under Michigan v. Bryant, a shooter at large with a gun threatens the public as well as the victim. Courts consider the location, the participants' purposes, the medical condition, and formality.
- B: Crawford left dying declarations open as a possibility. Bryant doesn't rest on that ground.
- C: Questioning at the scene isn't automatically testimonial. The primary purpose decides.
- D: Michigan v. Bryant shows that a description of a recent past event can still serve an ongoing emergency.
- E: Crawford covers out-of-court testimonial statements, such as police interrogations.
Rule: An ongoing emergency depends on the threat, and a gun with a shooter at large threatens the public. Courts weigh location, purposes, medical condition, and formality objectively (Michigan v. Bryant).

## Set 1 · Q22 · Confrontation, Bruton, and the Defense — Melendez-Diaz: lab certificates
In a drug trial, the prosecution offers a sworn lab certificate stating that seized pills are oxycodone. No analyst testifies, and the defense never had a chance to cross-examine one. The prosecution argues the test is neutral, routine science. What is the correct result?

A. Admitted, if the defense could have subpoenaed the analyst itself.
B. Admitted, because a record of regularly conducted activity is never testimonial.
C. Excluded, because the certificate is testimonial and no analyst was cross-examined.
D. Admitted, because scientific testing isn't accusatory like eyewitness testimony.
E. Excluded, because every lab report is inadmissible hearsay under the rules.

Answer: C
- A: The Clause puts the burden on the prosecution to present its witnesses.
- B: A sworn certificate made for trial is plainly an affidavit, whatever the routine.
- C: Correct. Under Melendez-Diaz, sworn lab certificates are testimonial, so the analyst must testify unless unavailable and previously cross-examined.
- D: Melendez-Diaz held forensic analysts are witnesses. Neutrality doesn't remove the Clause.
- E: The rule rests on the Confrontation Clause, not on a categorical hearsay bar.
Rule: Sworn lab certificates are testimonial, and forensic analysts are witnesses (Melendez-Diaz). A surrogate can't fill in (Bullcoming).

## Set 1 · Q23 · Confrontation, Bruton, and the Defense — Crawford: prior opportunity to cross
At a preliminary hearing, a witness testifies under oath, and defense counsel cross-examines her. She dies before trial. The prosecution offers a transcript of her testimony. Which is correct?

A. It is admissible if the judge finds that the testimony bears indicia of reliability.
B. It is barred, because the jury can't see the witness in person.
C. It is barred, because prior testimony is always testimonial.
D. It is admissible, because the Clause doesn't apply once a witness has died.
E. It is admissible, because the defense had a prior opportunity to cross-examine.

Answer: E
- A: That is the test of Ohio v. Roberts, which Crawford overruled for testimonial statements.
- B: Mattox allowed former testimony despite that loss.

Answer: B
- A: The proponent must prove the wrongdoing and the intent.

- D: The standard is a preponderance.
- E: Forfeiture is a preliminary question for the judge.

## Set 2 · Q20 · Confrontation, Bruton, and the Defense — Nontestimonial private remark
Facts: Nadia Voss is charged in federal court with burning down a warehouse she had insured. Investigators found a homemade chemical timer of an unusual design at the scene. Answer: B
- A: The remark to her son wasn't a report, and reporting duties don't make someone police (Ohio v. Clark).
- B: Correct. Under Ohio v. Clark, a statement to a family member with no primary purpose of creating trial testimony is nontestimonial, so only the hearsay rules apply (Whorton).

- E: The question concerns the nurse's own remark, a separate statement.

## Set 2 · Q21 · Confrontation, Bruton, and the Defense — Testifying witness with memory loss
A witness gave police a detailed statement but at trial takes the stand and says she remembers nothing about the event. The prosecution uses an admissible prior statement of hers. The defense argues confrontation is violated because she can't be meaningfully cross-examined. What is the most likely result?

A. No violation, because the Clause guarantees only an opportunity for effective cross.
B. A violation, because the statement to police was testimonial when she made it to them.
C. A violation, because a forgetful witness is treated as absent from the trial.
D. No violation, because police statements are nontestimonial.
E. A violation, unless she can answer every question on cross.

Answer: A
- A: Correct. United States v. Owens says the Clause guarantees an opportunity for effective cross-examination, not cross that is effective in whatever way the defense might wish. A declarant who testifies removes the problem (California v. Green).
- B: The statement may be testimonial, but the Clause doesn't bar statements the declarant is cross-examined about at trial.
- C: United States v. Owens rejected treating memory loss as absence.
- D: A statement to police describing past events is usually testimonial.
- E: The Clause doesn't require answers to every question.

## Set 2 · Q22 · Confrontation, Bruton, and the Defense — Bruton: prosecutors' options
A prosecutor plans a joint trial of Kerr and Lang. Kerr gave police a confession naming Lang. Which option does NOT avoid the Bruton problem?

A. Redacting all reference to Lang's existence from the text of the confession.
B. Severing the trials so that Lang is tried separately.
C. Using separate juries for the two defendants.
D. Having Kerr testify and submit to cross-examination.
E. Reading the confession with a clear limiting instruction.

Answer: E
- A: Richardson approves removing the defendant's name and any reference to his existence.
- B: Severance removes the confession from Lang's trial.
- C: Separate juries work the same way as severance.
- D: If the confessing codefendant testifies and can be crossed, the problem goes away.
- E: Correct. Bruton holds that a clear limiting instruction doesn't cure the problem, because the risk that jurors can't follow it is too great.
Rule: A limiting instruction doesn't cure a facially incriminating confession by a nontestifying codefendant (Bruton). Prosecutors may sever, use separate juries, have the codefendant testify, or redact (Richardson, Gray, Samia).

## Set 2 · Q23 · Confrontation, Bruton, and the Defense — Rule 106 as amended in 2023
