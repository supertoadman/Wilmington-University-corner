## Big picture
Start here. Every problem begins with government action and a clause. Religion claims run through coercion, Free Exercise, and the Establishment Clause; speech claims run through the framework, the unprotected categories, and the levels of scrutiny. Blue dashed arrows show where one doctrine hands off to another.

## Coercion: Barnette to Mahmoud
Chapter 1. Ask what the government is doing to the person: compelling a declaration, disciplining an employee's own prayer, or teaching a child something the parents' faith rejects.

## Free Exercise
Chapter 2's checklist, top to bottom: burden, neutrality and general applicability, Wrinkle 1, strict scrutiny, then Wrinkle 2. The table shows six ways a law fails Step 2.

## Establishment & funding
Chapters 1–2. Sort the government's action first: its own religious exercise, a private person's, an accommodation, or money. On this chart, green means the practice is permitted and red means it's forbidden.

## Speech framework
Chapter 3's speech checklist in order. Most cases turn on Step 4 (what kind of restriction) and Step 5 (whether the speech fits a historical category).

## Incitement & fighting words
Chapter 4. Name the harm the government fears; each fear has its own narrow test. Every case here held the speech protected.

## Prior restraints
Chapter 4. Stopping publication in advance carries a heavy presumption of invalidity, and the government has never overcome it on national-security grounds.

## Threats & crime speech
Chapter 5. What the speech is doing decides the test, and each test requires a culpable state of mind before the speech can be punished.

## Immoral speech
Chapter 6. Check the unprotected categories first (obscenity, child pornography), then ask whom the government is protecting: adults, kids, or neighborhoods.
