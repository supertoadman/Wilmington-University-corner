Evidence

Federal Rules of Evidence, Weeks 1–15 (Fisher, Evidence, 4th ed.): jury secrecy, relevance and 403, the policy exclusions, character evidence and 404(b), impeachment and the rape shield, hearsay and its exceptions, the Confrontation Clause, Bruton, the right to present a defense, lay and expert opinion, expert reliability, authentication and the best evidence rule, and privileges. Includes the outline, rule chart, flowcharts, flashcards, and bar-style practice questions.

Professional Responsibility

Model Rules of Professional Conduct, Weeks 1–15 (Burk, Ethical Lawyering, Chs. 1–25): regulating lawyers and bar admission, forming and ending the client-lawyer relationship, competence, confidentiality, agency and fiduciary duty, fees and client funds, conflicts of interest, government lawyers and prosecutors, ethics in advocacy, law firms and organizational clients, public service, duties to third parties, advertising and solicitation, and harassment and discrimination.

Con Law II

Constitutional Law II: the First Amendment, Weeks 1–15 (Chapters 1–13). Covers the Religion Clauses, the speech framework, incitement and national security, true threats and speech integral to crime, immoral speech, torts involving speech, commercial speech, content-neutral rules and expressive conduct, government speech, funding and forum doctrine, government employees and public schools, and social media and elections.

Sales and Leases

Sales and Leases of Goods under UCC Articles 1, 2 and 2A, Weeks 1–14 (Sepinuck, Chapters 1–7, 9 and 11). Covers which law governs, formation and the battle of the forms, the statute of frauds, warranties and disclaimers, gap fillers, delivery and risk of loss, the parol evidence rule, assignment and delegation, repudiation, excuse, buyer's and seller's remedies, rejection, cure and revocation, privity, and leases under Article 2A.

Agency & Partnership

Business Associations, Weeks 1–8 (Bainbridge, Park & Rodrigues, 6th ed.). Covers creating an agency, actual and apparent authority, ratification and estoppel, a principal's tort liability, agents' fiduciary duties, choosing an entity, partnership formation under the UPA and RUPA, partners' rights, authority and duties, dissociation and dissolution, loss sharing, buyout agreements, limited partnerships, and LLCs.

Contracts I

Contracts I, Weeks 1–9 (Epstein, Markell & Ponoroff, Making and Doing Deals, 7th ed.). Covers whether there is an enforceable agreement, the objective theory of assent, offers and their termination, options and firm offers, methods of acceptance and silence, the mirror-image rule and UCC § 2-207, misunderstanding, indefiniteness and letters of intent, consideration, modification, promissory estoppel, the statute of frauds, duress, and misrepresentation.

Civil Procedure

Civil Procedure, Weeks 1–9 (Spencer, Civil Procedure: A Contemporary Approach, 7th ed., and the Federal Civil Rules Supplement). Covers the court systems and sources of procedural law, personal jurisdiction from Pennoyer through Ford, notice and service of process under Rule 4, federal question, diversity, supplemental and removal jurisdiction, venue, transfer and forum non conveniens, the Erie doctrine through Shady Grove, pleading standards from Conley to Iqbal, Rule 11, and responding to the complaint under Rules 8, 12, and 55.

Torts

Torts, Weeks 1–15 (Prosser, Wade & Schwartz, Torts: Cases and Materials, keyed to the 13th ed.). Covers liability based on fault, the intentional torts and their privileges, negligence (the reasonable person, professionals, negligence per se, proof, and res ipsa loquitur), cause in fact and proximate cause, duty and the failure to act, comparative fault, assumption of risk, limitations and immunities, premises liability, compensatory and punitive damages, joint and several liability, contribution, vicarious liability, strict liability, and nuisance.

    Evidence Concepts Test

    A 116-question concepts test on the whole course, Weeks 1–15, in outline order: jury secrecy, relevance and 403, the policy rules (407–411), character evidence, 404(b), and 413–415, impeachment and the rape shield, the hearsay definition, 801(d), and the 803–807 exceptions, the Confrontation Clause through Smith v. Each question turns on a holding, an element, or a recent amendment, and its wrong choices are the dissents, overruled cases, and older rules students mix up. Five choices each. Tutor mode grades as you go; timed mode gives you 90 seconds a question. Every question has the rule, an analysis, and why each choice is right or wrong.
